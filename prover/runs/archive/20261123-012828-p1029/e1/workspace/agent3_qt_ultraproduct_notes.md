# Finite-AW quasitracial ultraproduct: auxiliary construction

This is an auxiliary lemma, NOT a proof of the problem's exclusion.
It uses the standard projection dimension/comparison theory of finite AW* factors.

For each factor M_k let d_k(p)=tau_k(p). For bounded x define
rho_k(x)=inf {epsilon+d_k(p): p projection, ||x(1-p)||<=epsilon}.
Intersect good projections to get the triangle inequality. For ||x||<=K,
rho_k(x)<=epsilon+tau_k(x*x)/epsilon^2.
Conversely ||x(1-p)||<=epsilon implies
 d_k(1_(2epsilon,infinity)(|x|))<=d_k(p),
by intersecting this high spectral projection with ker(p). Consequently
 tau_k(x*x)<=4epsilon^2+K^2 d_k(p).
Thus rho-null and L2-quasitrace-null uniformly bounded sequences coincide.

Let B=product_k M_k and I={x:lim_U tau_k(x_k*x_k)=0}.
Finite sums are controlled by rho's triangle inequality; left/right products
are controlled by positivity and tau(z*z)=tau(zz*). Thus I is a closed
2-sided ideal. On bounded selfadjoint sets, spectral interlacing yields
 |tau(a)-tau(b)| <= epsilon + 2K*tau((a-b)^2)/epsilon^2
for ||a||,||b||<=K. Indeed split a-b into a norm-epsilon term and a
term of support dimension at most tau((a-b)^2)/epsilon^2, then integrate
rank-interlacing bounds on spectral distributions. Hence ultralimit tau
is well-defined on Q=B/I. It is faithful by definition of I.
The same construction on M_2(M_k) gives the 2-extension: its null ideal
is exactly M_2(I), using corner-compression monotonicity and finite sums.

All uniformly bounded rho-balls in Q are complete by the usual metric
ultraproduct diagonal argument. Projection lifting holds by taking the
1/2 spectral cut of a positive contraction lift, since pointwise
 |h-cut_(1/2)(h)| <= 2 |h^2-h|.
Every element also has a unitary polar factorization: complete each
component polar partial isometry to a unitary using finite-factor
projection cancellation, then pass to Q.

An increasing sequence of projections p_n is rho-Cauchy because
 tau(p_m-p_n)=tau(p_m)-tau(p_n). Its limit is a projection and its join.
For arbitrary x in Q, choose a lift x_k and use joins of the projections
represented by 1_(1/n,infinity)(|x_k|). Their join p satisfies xp=x;
xy=0 forces each cut times y to vanish and hence py=0. Thus p is the
right support of x. Finite projection joins follow by taking support
of sums. Arbitrary projection joins reduce to a countable collection
of finite joins whose dimensions approach the supremum of all finite
join dimensions. A projection not below that countable join would
increase dimension, a contradiction. Thus Q is AW*.

For factoriality, a central projection p of dimension theta in (0,1)
lifts to projections p_k. Choose partial isometries v_k taking a
subprojection of p_k to one of 1-p_k of common component dimension
min(d_k(p_k),1-d_k(p_k)). Their quotient v is nonzero and satisfies
pv=0, vp=v, contradicting centrality. The center of an AW* algebra is
generated by projections, so Z(Q)=C. Finally, if v*v=1, then tau(vv*)=1,
so tau(1-vv*)=0 and faithfulness gives vv*=1. Thus Q is finite.

Norm-algebraic facts needing no AW proof: product B, and therefore every
C* quotient, has stable rank one. Approximate x_k=u_k|x_k| by
u_k(|x_k|+epsilon), whose inverses have uniform norm <=1/epsilon.
The product and all quotients also have real rank zero by common-grid
rounding of component selfadjoints, and exact unitary polar factorizations.
These properties alone do NOT imply hyponormal rigidity: see agent2's
norm-matrix-quotient counterexample.
