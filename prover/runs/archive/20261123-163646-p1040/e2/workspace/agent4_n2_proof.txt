Equality for n=2.

Use the standard Nielsen generating theorem and standard presentation
GL(2,Z)=<S,T,R | S^4=1, (ST)^3=S^2, R^2=1, RSR=S^-1, RTR=T^-1>.
Take S(x)=y,S(y)=x^-1; T(x)=x,T(y)=xy; R(x)=x^-1,R(y)=y.
Direct free-word computation proves S^4=R^2=RSRS=RTRT=1,
(ST)^3 S^-2 = conjugation by y x^-1.
Nielsen's generators (inversion, interchange, transvection) lie in the subgroup
generated by S,T,R: RS interchanges x,y, and S^2 gives simultaneous inversion.
Consequently the map GL(2,Z) -> Aut(F2)/Inn(F2) given by these lifts is
well-defined and surjective; its composite with abelianization is identity.
Thus IA_2=Inn(F2).

For any free group F of rank >=2 and any r>=1,
Z(F/gamma_{r+1}F)=gamma_r F/gamma_{r+1}F.
Indeed containment >= is immediate. Otherwise choose a representative
w in gamma_d minus gamma_{d+1}, with d<r. Magnus-Witt identifies its
initial term with a nonzero homogeneous element u in the free Lie ring.
Centrality implies [u,X_i]=0 for every i. Embed the free Lie ring in the
free associative ring. A homogeneous polynomial p of positive degree
commuting with X_1 is a multiple of X_1^d: the equation pX_1=X_1p
forces every word in its support to start in X_1, then cancel that first
letter and induct. Such a polynomial cannot also commute with X_2
unless zero. Contradiction.
(The choice d exists among 1,...,r-1 simply if w is not in gamma_r.)

Now conjugation by w acts trivially on F2/gamma_{r+1} iff its residue is
central iff w belongs to gamma_r. As F2 has trivial center, Inn:F2->IA2
is an isomorphism, and therefore
A_r(2)=Inn(gamma_r F2)=gamma_r(IA2).
The requested intersection equality follows for every c.
