Private-external-pair symmetry lemma for rank6, cover6, six cores of size4, one external point p=24.
Every point z of every member F is private against some member E: F is itself a cover of size6 (intersecting), while F minus z has size5 and cannot cover. Thus E intersects F exactly at z.
After anchoring F=X0+{4,p}, choose its private witness for p, G. Then G must contain p, cannot own core0 (it would share X0), and cannot own core1 (it would share4). Hence its owner lies in {2,3,4,5}; its other petalpoint y lies outside X0, that owncore, and {4,p}. It either lies in X1 minus4, or in one of the three other unanchored cores. The stabilizer of F and the core partition has exactly two orbits, represented by G=X2+{p,5} and G=X2+{p,12}. Python explicitly enumerates all60 possibilities and supplies a genuine core-preserving permutation fixing F to the stated representative: orbit sizes12 and48.
Therefore testing the two anchored branches is complete, not heuristic. The lemma does not decide either branch, and does not address configurations with more external vertices.
