# Integral injectivity in degree 2, all ranks

Let H=Z^n, V=H^* tensor Lambda^2 H, Q=Lambda^2 V, T=H^* tensor L_3(H), and B:Q->T be derivation bracket. Magnus generation and the first Johnson map identify IA_n^ab=V. Consequently the true relation lattice R_n=ker(Q->gr^gamma_2 IA_n) lies in ker B. Any actual group relation with zero exponent sums yields an element of R_n by taking its class-two free-group signature, and R_n is stable under GL_n(Z).

## Exact finite certificates
`agent2_source_relations.py` enumerates reduced words in the standard Magnus automorphisms. It represents their action by exact reduced free words, identifies equal automorphisms, and records differences of class-two signatures. Thus every column it outputs is an actual relation in R_n; completeness of the enumeration is unnecessary.

For n=3, radii<=5 give a primitive rank15 relation lattice. `agent2_gl_closure.py` closes it under elementary GL3 actions and obtains primitive rank18 in Q of rank36. It verifies B kills it and rank(B)=18. Hence R_3=ker B.

For n=4, radius<=3 gives primitive rank102. GL4 closure has ranks190,206,206 in Q of rank276. Smith invariants all206 ones; exact rank(B)=70. Hence R_4=ker B.

`agent2_sparse_closure.py` embeds the rank4 relation basis on all four-element subsets and adds all pairs of Magnus automorphisms that commute (commuting is checked by exact free-word multiplication). It closes under elementary GL_n actions using integral unit-pivot elimination. Only reductions with pivot+/-1 are accepted; discarded candidates cannot invalidate the certificate. Every accepted row is therefore an actual group relation. Pivot ones imply its span is primitive. For n=5 it gets rank1040 and verifies Johnson rank185, sum1225=rank Q. For n=6 it gets rank3606 and verifies Johnson rank399, sum4005=rank Q. It directly checks B kills every source-relation basis row. Thus R_n=ker B for 3<=n<=6. For n=2 this follows from IA2=Inn(F2).

All files are self-contained except imports of our own helpers and SymPy. Runtime: n3 enumeration6sec; n3 closure<1sec; n4 enumeration/closure9sec; n5/6 sparse closure+verification2sec.

## General locality lemma: ker B is generated on at most six indices

Define the Morita trace t:T->S^2 H by embedding L3 in H^{tensor3}, contracting the dual root with the first tensor slot, and symmetrizing the remaining pair. For a root dual e_r^*,
 t(e_r^* tensor [[Xa,Xb],Xc])=delta(r,a) Xb Xc-delta(r,b) Xa Xc.
This formula shows directly tB=0. It also shows t is onto for n>=2: for XaXb choose r!=a and use e_r^* tensor [[Xr,Xa],Xb].

Take the usual integral Hall basis of L3, consisting of left-normed brackets [[Xi,Xj],Xk] with i!=j and the Hall order condition. Each basis vector of T has trace either0 or +/-one monomial XaXb. Therefore ker t has the following integral basis: every zero-trace Hall vector; and, for each monomial XaXb, signed differences between the Hall vectors tracing to that monomial and one chosen representative b0(a,b). Each such kernel-basis vector is supported on at most four indices (a,b and two possible root labels). Every representative b0(a,b) has support contained in {a,b,r0(a,b)}.

In ranks2,3,4 the bracket image equals ker t integrally. This follows by the exact sparse unit-pivot bracket-rank certificates (ranks1,18,70): the accepted primitive image sublattice has the same rational rank as ker t, which is saturated, so they agree. Thus each of the just-described ker t basis elements has a B-preimage using only the labels of that basis element, at most four. Choose one such preimage for each basis element. This gives an integral linear section s:ker t->Q.

Now take a basis vector c of Q, a wedge of two elementary derivations. It uses at most six labels. If Bc!=0, then one derivation's root must equal one of the other's leaves; hence c uses at most five distinct labels. B respects the Z^n weight (leaf counts minus root counts), so Bc is weight homogeneous.

If that weight has a negative coordinate, every Hall term of Bc has trace zero. The chosen s(Bc) is supported on labels already present in c.

If the weight is nonnegative, it is exactly the degree-two monomial XaXb. In the above ker t basis, expansion of Bc uses only Hall vectors already present in Bc together with the single chosen representative b0(a,b). Consequently s(Bc) is supported on the support of c together with the one additional root r0(a,b).

In either case, c-s(Bc) belongs to ker B and uses at most six labels. If Bc=0 the same conclusion holds by taking c itself. Finally, for x=sum_c a_c c in ker B,
 x=sum_c a_c(c-s(Bc)),
because s is linear and sum a_c Bc=0. This proves ker B is generated by the kernels restricted to sets of at most six labels.

Since those local kernels are actual source relations by the finite certificates, ker B=R_n for EVERY n>=2. Equivalently gamma_2(IA_n) intersection A_3(n)=gamma_3(IA_n); so (n,2) is excluded from D for every n.
