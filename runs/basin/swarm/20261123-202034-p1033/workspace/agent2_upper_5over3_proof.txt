THEOREM. For all integers t>=2, b(t) <= t(3 ceil(t^(1/3))^2 - ceil(t^(1/3)) - 2). In particular limsup log b(t)/log t <=5/3.

Construction on T=h^3 classes (h>=2). Let n=2h^2 and let G be the disjoint union of h copies of K_{h,h}. Thus G is h-regular, has n vertices and T=h^3 edges. Classes are indexed by owner edges ab of G. Let W=E(K_n) be an actual ground-point set disjoint from all the cores. Write St(a) for the n-1 edges of K_n incident with a. Put r=n-h-1 and l=h^2-1. For class i=ab, define P_i to consist of every r-subset of St(a)\{ab} and every r-subset of St(b)\{ab}. Each P has a designated center a or b (indeed r>=2 makes it unique).

If P is centered at a, let O(P)={c!=a:ac notin P}; then |O(P)|=h. Let S(P) be the G-edges incident with at least one vertex of O(P), except the owner i. Since the other endpoint of i belongs to O(P), the owner was counted, so |S(P)|<=h^2-1=l. Extend S(P) arbitrarily to a set T(P) of exactly l owner classes excluding i. This is possible because T-1>=l.

Take pairwise disjoint cores X_i, each of size T, disjoint from W. For each P in P_i, include ALL sets X_i union P union {x_j:j in T(P)}, choosing x_j arbitrarily in X_j independently. Every member has cardinality k=T+r+l, and k-|X_i|=D=r+l=3h^2-h-2. A member of class i contains all X_i and at most one point of each other core, so the classes are pairwise disjoint as families.

Intersection. Same-class members share X_i. For distinct classes, if actual petals P,Q intersect, done. Otherwise their centers a,c cannot coincide, since 2r>n-1. Their only possible common actual point is ac, so ac is omitted from P or Q. In the first case c belongs to O(P), and Q's owner class (a G-edge incident with c) belongs to T(P). Hence the first member meets the entire core contained in the second member. The other case is symmetric.

Covering number. For a subset A of actual points W, let J(A) be the classes whose entire petal family P_i is met by A. If i=ab is in J(A), then A meets every r-subset of St(a)\{ab} and St(b)\{ab}. Since these ground sets have size n-2, both intersections with A have size at least (n-2)-r+1=h. In particular deg_A(a),deg_A(b)>=h. Let Z={a:deg_A(a)>=h}. Then |J(A)|<=e_G(Z)<=h|Z|/2<=|A| by regularity of G and the handshake lemma in the graph (V,A).

Now suppose C is a cover with |C|<T. Let A=C cap W, and B={i:C cap X_i nonempty}. Every core has T>|C| points. If i is outside B and some P in P_i avoids A, choose every target point x_j outside C; the resulting member avoids C, contradiction. Thus all T-|B| classes outside B belong to J(A), so T-|B|<=|A| and |C|>=|B|+|A|>=T, contradiction. One arbitrary representative from each core is a T-cover, so tau=T.

Arbitrary t. For h=ceil(t^(1/3)), T=h^3>=t. Retain any t of the T classes, with their existing cores and rank k. This subfamily has covering number exactly t: a cover of size<t, supplemented by one core representative from each omitted class, would cover the full construction with fewer than T points. Consequently b(t)<=tD. Since h=t^(1/3)+O(1), this proves the claimed exponent 5/3 upper bound.
