Owner-labelled sunflower compression lemma.
Assume disjoint cores Xi, residual ranks <=d, all original members pairwise intersecting. For P in class i, set E(P)=P union {label(j):P meets Xj}. Suppose E(P_1),...,E(P_L) is an ordinary sunflower, L>=d+2, with kernel K union labels(J), where K consists of actual vertices. Remove from J all owners of points in K, obtaining J0. For arbitrary choices y_j in Xj (j in J0), add G=Xi union K union {y_j:j in J0}. Its residual size <=|K|+|J0|<=d_i, because each original P must contain K and meet every Xj for j in J0, with these requirements requiring distinct vertices.

Claim G intersects every original member F in class h. If F meets Xi or K, immediate. If h in J, G meets Xh (either through K or y_h). Otherwise at most one sunflower branch has a point in Xh, since owner-label h is outside the kernel. Of remaining branches, if F misses Xi and K, each original Xi union P_l must intersect F through an actual tail point in F\Xh. These tails are pairwise disjoint and |F\Xh|<=d. There are L-1>=d+1 branches, impossible.

All additions, even from different sunflower choices and classes, are pairwise intersecting. Suppose added G_i and G_h disjoint. Its kernel data implies h notin J_i and K_i disjoint G_h; Xi also disjoint G_h. At most one branch of the first sunflower meets Xh, and at most d branches meet G_h\Xh. L>=d+2 leaves a branch whose original edge is disjoint G_h, contradicting the preceding claim that every added member meets every original member. Additions within the same class share Xi.

Adding these edges preserves covering number t: the old family is included, so tau cannot decrease; one core representative per class still covers, so tau cannot exceed t. This is valid for nonuniform residual rank <=d_i. After padding each Xi to cardinality >=t, covers of cardinality <t of a full product addition must hit Xi or K (unless hitting an actually fixed point already in K); they cannot exhaust an arbitrary target core Xj.
