GENERAL REDUCTION.
Let V=Z^n, T^=prod_{d>=0} V^{tensor d}, I=T^_{>=1}. Magnus embeds
F_n into 1+I by x_i -> 1+X_i. Its dimension-subgroup theorem says
F_n intersect (1+I^s)=gamma_s(F_n), and identifies the homogeneous
leading terms with the integral free Lie algebra L_s(V).
If alpha in A_r, the induced substitution Phi_alpha has
(Phi_alpha-id)(I^d) subset I^{d+r}. Conversely this condition for d=1
implies alpha in A_r. If alpha in A_r and beta in A_s, then
Phi_[alpha,beta]-id=(Phi_alpha Phi_beta-Phi_beta Phi_alpha)
 Phi_alpha^{-1} Phi_beta^{-1}
raises degree by r+s. Therefore [A_r,A_s] subset A_{r+s}.
In particular gamma_c(IA_n) subset A_c.
For alpha in A_c define tau_c(alpha)(e_i) as the homogeneous
term of degree c+1 of Magnus(alpha(x_i)x_i^{-1}) (equivalently
alpha(x_i)-x_i in group-algebra coordinates). These terms lie in
L_{c+1}(V), and leading-order composition gives an additive map
A_c -> Hom(V,L_{c+1}(V)) with kernel A_{c+1}.
Thus it induces
j_c: gamma_c(IA_n)/gamma_{c+1}(IA_n) -> Hom(V,L_{c+1}(V)),
whose kernel is exactly
(gamma_c(IA_n) intersect A_{c+1})/gamma_{c+1}(IA_n).
This proves the non-strict inclusion in every case and reformulates
strictness as nonzero kernel of j_c. Since the target is torsion-free,
any nonzero torsion class in gr_c(IA_n) suffices for strictness;
the converse is NOT asserted.
