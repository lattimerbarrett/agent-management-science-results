# A two-terminal degree-four resistance bound
Let H be a simple two-connected graph on b vertices, with distinct marked s,t. Suppose every vertex other than s,t has degree at least four. Then R_H(s,t) <= (b-1)/4.

Take a shortest cycle C through s,t (exists by two-connectivity). If it omits a vertex, its two arc lengths p,q sum to at most b-1, and Rayleigh gives R_H <= pq/(p+q) <= (b-1)/4. Otherwise C is Hamiltonian. Write its oriented s-to-t arcs as s,A_1,...,A_r,t and s,B_1,...,B_l,t. A chord with endpoints on one arc would shorten C through s,t, so all chords join A to B, and every internal vertex has at least two such chords. In particular neither arc has zero internal vertices. The vertex A_1 has neighbors B_j,B_k with j<k. Vertex B_j has another cross-neighbor A_h (h>1). The chords A_1 B_k and A_h B_j yield an s,t cycle omitting h-2 A-vertices and k-j-1 B-vertices, so shortestness forces h=2 and k=j+1. Thus the graph includes cross edges A_1 B_j, A_1 B_(j+1), A_2 B_j, forming two triangles sharing A_1 B_j and using the C edges A_1 A_2 and B_j B_(j+1).

On C alone take optimal unit s-to-t flow, alpha on its A arc and beta on its B arc, alpha+beta=1. Divert x units of A-edge current through A_1-B_j-A_2 and y units of B-edge current through B_j-A_1-B_(j+1). The shared chord carries x-y, the other new chords carry x,y. Relative energy decrease is 2 alpha x+2 beta y-3x^2-3y^2+2xy. Choosing x=(3alpha+beta)/8, y=(alpha+3beta)/8 gives saving (3alpha^2+2alpha beta+3beta^2)/8 = 1/4+(2alpha-1)^2/8 >=1/4. Thus R_H <= R_C-1/4 <=(b-1)/4. This is an explicit valid flow, so Thomson's principle gives the result without any hidden optimizer assumption.

## Extra quarter-unit defect when the shortest cycle is Hamiltonian
In the exact degree pattern (s,t degree two, every other vertex degree four), a Hamiltonian shortest cycle has chord graph two-regular between its two sets of internal arc vertices. Consequently both sides have the same size r. For r=2 the graph is the six-vertex K4-core module. For r>=3 there are two edge-disjoint beneficial diamond pairs: one constructed at A_1 as above, with edges A_1 B_j,A_1 B_(j+1),A_2 B_j, and one at A_r, with edges A_r B_k,A_r B_(k+1),A_(r-1) B_(k+1). The two A-cycle edges differ. If the B-cycle edges were the same, B_(j+1) would have the three distinct neighbors A_1,A_r,A_(r-1), contradiction. Crossedge overlap could only involve A_2=A_(r-1), hence r=3, and would force B_j=B_(k+1); this vertex would then have neighbors A_1,A_2,A_3, another contradiction. Thus the two circulation corrections have disjoint edge supports, so their savings add to at least one half. Hence R <= b/4-1/2=(b-2)/4.

## Complete Hamiltonian-shortest-cycle classification
Suppose again that the exact degree pattern holds. Set r=|A|=|B|; the chord graph is two-regular. The no-spaced-inversion rule says edges A_i B_l,A_h B_j with i<h,j<l force h=i+1,l=j+1. It forces a consecutive decomposition into zipper cycles, as follows. Number from zero in this paragraph. The first A_0 has neighbors B_j,B_(j+1). If j>0, either neighbor A_h of B_0 together with A_0 B_(j+1) violates the inversion rule; so j=0. The other neighbor of B_0 is A_1. Then A_1's second neighbor is B_l with l>=1. If l=1, this closes a K2,2 component. If l>1, B_1's second neighbor A_h has h>1, so the inversion rule forces h=l=2. Continue: A_i's known neighbor is B_(i-1), its other neighbor B_l has l>=i. If l=i the current component closes. Otherwise B_i has second neighbor A_h with h>i, and inversion forces h=l=i+1. When a component closes all its vertices have degree two, so restart on the remaining consecutive intervals. Thus each component of k>=2 levels has crossedges A_0 B_0,A_(k-1)B_(k-1), and A_i B_(i+1),A_(i+1)B_i for 0<=i<k-1.

Swapping A_i and B_i for every i preserves the full graph (including cycle edges), s,t. Therefore its electrical potentials are equal on each paired level. Initial/final attachment resistance is 1/2 each. Consecutive levels in one zipper component are joined by four edges (resistance 1/4); at boundaries between components there are only the two original cycle edges (resistance 1/2). If there are h components and r levels, exact resistance is 1+(r-h)/4+(h-1)/2 = 1/2+r/4+h/4. Since every component has at least two levels, h<=floor(r/2). With b=2r+2 this gives R<=(3b+2)/16, with exact sharper value 3b/16 for b divisible by four.
