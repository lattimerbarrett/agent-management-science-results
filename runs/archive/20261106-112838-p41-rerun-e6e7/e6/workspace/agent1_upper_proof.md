# Complete upper-bound proof

For every d>=3 and k>=1, b(d,k)<=d-2.

Lemma 1 (skeletons and prefix orders). Every pure skeleton of a k-decomposable complex is k-decomposable. Every pure k-decomposable complex has a facet order all of whose nonempty prefixes are k-decomposable. For skeletons, use the same shedding sigma if it fits in the skeleton, otherwise the skeleton equals the skeleton of the deletion; the simplex case is a uniform skeleton, which is vertex-decomposable. For prefixes, order the deletion recursively, then append sigma times the recursively ordered link facets. At every latter prefix sigma remains a shedding face.

Lemma 2 (homology facets). If X is pure k-decomposable and F is a missing top-dimensional face with every proper subset in X, then X union 2^F is k-decomposable. Induct on the number of faces of X. In dimension zero, all complexes are vertex-decomposable. A positive-dimensional simplex admits no such F. Otherwise choose a shedding sigma. If sigma is not contained in F, deletion gains the same homology facet F and link stays unchanged. If sigma is contained in F, deletion stays unchanged and link gains the homology facet F\sigma. Each changed complex is covered by induction, and deletion remains pure of the original dimension. Note sigma=F is impossible.

Lemma 3 (relative cones). Let L be a pure q-dimensional k-decomposable complex and a a fresh vertex. Let T be any collection of (q+2)-element sets F with every proper subset in L. Then C=(a*L) union union_{F in T}2^F is k-decomposable. If q=-1, C is a nonempty discrete vertex set. If L is a simplex and q>=0, T is empty, so C is a simplex. Otherwise take a shedding sigma of L. The deletion of C is the relative cone over del_sigma L, with precisely those T-facets avoiding sigma. Its link is the relative cone over lk_sigma L, with precisely the facets F\sigma for F in T containing sigma. Proper faces of an F containing sigma that avoid sigma already lie in del_sigma L; similarly the new link facets have boundaries in lk_sigma L. Apply induction on the number of faces of L. The deletion is pure of dimension q+1 since it contains the cone over the pure q-dimensional deletion of L and its other facets have the same size.

Base dimension two. The graph of a pure k-decomposable 2-complex X is connected. This follows either from its shellability, or directly by induction: the deletion graph is connected, and each removed facet meets the deletion along a nonempty edge. While the graph is not complete, choose an induced length-two path x-y-z and add triangle xyz. The new edge xz is a shedding face: deleting it recovers X, and its link is the simplex on {y}. This is allowed for k>=1. Repeat until the graph is complete. All remaining missing triangles now have full boundary present, so add them in any order by Lemma 2. This proves the zero-auxiliary-vertex completion theorem in dimension two.

Inductive construction in dimension d>=3. Choose A={a} disjoint union B, with |B|=d-3. Let L_0 be the (d-1)-skeleton of X. By Lemma 1 it is k-decomposable and has an all-prefix k-decomposable facet order G_1,...,G_m. Add the facets {a} union G_j in this order. At each step a sheds: its deletion is X and its link is the corresponding prefix of L_0. Thus we reach X union (a*L_0).

By the dimension induction hypothesis complete L_0 on V union B by a sequence L_0,L_1,...,L_s of k-decomposable complexes, adding one d-element facet at a time. Lift each addition G to the d-dimensional facet {a} union G. Every resulting complex is X union (a*L_i), k-decomposable by Lemma 3 because the boundary of every original facet of X already lies in L_0. At the end L_s is the full (d-1)-skeleton on V union B. Finally add all still-missing (d+1)-element facets avoiding a, in arbitrary order. Lemma 3 again applies, since every such facet has its entire boundary in L_s.

The resulting order contains exactly all missing (d+1)-element facets on V union A: those containing a occur in the first two phases, and those avoiding a in the final phase. Vertices in A first occur only through added facets. Every intermediate is k-decomposable, and |A|=d-2, proving the upper bound.
